CASE BRIEFING

What the recovered phone records

Noor Khalil is a 24-year-old art researcher living in Paris. Her phone is recovered near the Seine after her final outgoing message on Monday night. It contains seven pieces of evidence spread across Messages, Photos, Maps, Notes, and voicemail. The interface preserves the original timestamps, so a claim made at 8:55 PM can be checked against a photograph taken at 8:58 PM or a route saved at 9:14 PM.

The case focuses on three people in Noor’s recent history. Sara Okonkwo is her closest friend and receives the last message at 9:11 PM. Adam Reiss says he spends the evening at a hotel, while Noor’s camera roll places him outside Saint-Louis Clinic. Dr. Chen confirms that Noor leaves the clinic at 8:10 PM by choice. These statements do not all describe the same sequence of events, and the differences matter.

How the investigation works

Open each phone app and pin evidence that changes the timeline, establishes a location, or tests someone’s account. The board updates as clues are collected. One private note uses a four-digit date in day-month order; both parts of that date appear elsewhere on the phone. The final theory becomes available after five clues, including Sara’s conversation, the saved route, and the private note.

You can investigate alone without an account, but guest progress stays in the current browser tab. Signing in stores a case in the connected Supabase database. Private cooperative rooms allow up to six signed-in players to investigate from different devices. Pinned evidence, room notes, hints, and the solved state synchronize for every member. An invite code is the key to a room, so share it only with the people joining your case.

This is a fictional mystery. Noor, Sara, Adam, Dr. Chen, their messages, and the depicted locations belong to the game. Start with the phone notification, check each timestamp, and submit a theory only when the route and the witness accounts agree.
